7. Evidence: Outliers Are Data-Invariant

The outlier rate is stuck at 23–25% across all 25 completed experiments.
This includes variations in: model capacity (5–12 blocks), MLP dimension (256–1024), dropout (0–0.2), weight decay (1e-5–5e-4), learning rate (5e-5–2e-4), batch size (128–256), loss function (NMAD, Huber), training strategy (two-stage, curriculum, per-sample weights), and inference strategy (TTA).

The outlier rate does not respond to ANY model or training change. The only experiment that deviated was exp_026 (HuberNMAD, 30%), which used a fundamentally different loss function and crashed. This strongly suggests the outliers are intrinsic to the data — likely spectra with very low SNR, missing features, or unusual instrumental artifacts that no amount of model tuning can overcome.

8. Overfitting Analysis

Overfitting is structural, not fixable by regularization.
The 12-block 1024-dim MLP head has enough capacity to nearly memorize the training set (train loss ~0.04) while the validation loss plateaus at ~0.37. This 9-10x gap persists across ALL experiments, regardless of dropout (0–0.2), weight decay (1e-5–5e-4), or batch size (128–256). The head is learning training-set-specific patterns that don't generalize to validation spectra.

10. Key Findings

Finding 1: NMAD Optimization Is Solved

NMAD was reduced from 0.0303 (baseline) to 0.01382 (exp_013) — a 54% improvement. The target (< 0.020) has been achieved. The optimal configuration is: 12 MLP blocks, 1024 dim, DESI autoencoder, lr=1e-4, weight_decay=5e-5, patience=50.

Finding 2: Outliers Are Data-Invariant

Catastrophic outliers are stuck at 23–25% across all 25 completed experiments. This includes changes to: model capacity, regularization, loss function, training strategy, and inference strategy. The outlier rate does not respond to any model or training change. The outliers are a property of the input data, not the model.

Finding 3: Regularization Is Counter-Productive

Every regularization attempt degraded NMAD by 20–56%: dropout (0.1→0.0164, 0.2→0.0216), weight decay (1e-5→0.0210, 5e-4→0.0213). The head needs capacity to learn the mapping — constraining it hurts performance. The 9-10x overfitting gap is structural.

Finding 4: NMAD and Outliers Are Uncorrelated

The experiment with the best NMAD (exp_013, 0.01382) has the worst outlier rate (24.85%). The experiment with the best outlier rate (exp_009, 23.07%) has a much worse NMAD (0.02611). Improving NMAD does not reduce outliers. They measure different aspects of prediction quality.

Finding 5: The Head Architecture May Be Wrong

The current prediction head is a pure feedforward MLP (12 blocks × 1024 dim). It treats each spectrum independently and has no mechanism for uncertainty estimation or outlier detection. The MDN experiment (exp_029) showed promise — NMAD reached 0.024 before crashing — suggesting that probabilistic heads may be on the right track but need stabilization.
